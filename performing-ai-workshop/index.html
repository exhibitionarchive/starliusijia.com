Performing AI

A Critical Making Workshop on Perceived AI Aesthetics

In-person workshop · CHI 2027 · Workshop date TBA
About the Workshop

As AI-generated content becomes increasingly incorporated into creative fields, creative practitioners have begun to develop their own expectations about what makes a visual work appear “AI-generated.” These expectations are not fixed, and different creators may recognize, reproduce, or challenge different visual characteristics.

This workshop explores perceived AI aesthetics through a critical reverse process. Rather than focusing on human–AI co-creation, we ask what happens when human creators intentionally make work that appears AI-generated. Through blind critique, collective discussion, and rapid visual prototyping, participants will examine how AI-like visual characteristics are recognized, shared, negotiated, and potentially developed into a broader aesthetic language.

The workshop brings together creative practitioners and HCI researchers interested in AI, creativity, and visual culture. It aims to open discussion around visual judgment, creative authorship, stereotypes around AI-generated work, and the changing relationship between human creative practice and generative technologies.

Workshop Focus
01

Recognize

What makes a visual work appear AI-generated to creative practitioners?

02

Compare

Where do creators' intended AI-like characteristics align with or differ from others' judgments?

03

Make

How can perceived AI characteristics be translated into rapid human-made visual prototypes?

04

Reflect

Can characteristics associated with generative AI become an aesthetic language beyond indicators of production?
